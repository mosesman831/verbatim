## A. How cat-5 is SUPPOSED to be scored

**Official LoCoMo eval (answer side)** — `snap-research/locomo` `task_eval/evaluation.py` (~lines 199-237, [vendor], https://github.com/snap-research/locomo/blob/master/task_eval/evaluation.py): category 5 is scored by substring match — the model answer is correct iff it contains `"no information available"` or `"not mentioned"`; anything else scores 0. The dataset's per-question `answer`/`adversarial_answer` field is the *tempting wrong answer*, never the target. So the answer-side contract is **refusal output, not corrected answer**.

**Official LoCoMo eval (retrieval side)** — same file: `recall_acc` is computed over each question's `evidence` turn ids *for every category including cat-5*. The gold evidence for cat-5 = the premise-related turns the trap was built from (speaker/attribute the question re-binds). A retrieval engine is therefore expected to **still surface premise turns** — the evidence ids stay meaningful under adversarial questions.

**Verbatim v7 harness semantics** — `eval/v7/corpora.py:890-1024` `load_locomo` [code]: `adversarial = cat_id == 5` → `answerable=not adversarial` (line ~970), and `evidence_ids` carry the premise turns with a comment "premise-related turns used for speaker-mismatch checks (V7-24.01), not answer support". `CorpusTask` docstring (~corpora.py:218-219 [code]): "delivered items are then scored by the refusal metric, not by recall" — **but that is not literally what the metrics do**: `metrics.py` `aggregate()` computes `any@k` over `scored = [r for r in recs if r.gold]` (metrics.py:~336 [code]), and cat-5 gold is non-empty, so per-category `any@k` DOES count premise-turn hits. Simultaneously `correct_refusal_rate` (metrics.py:292-300 [code]) credits `not answerable` records where `withheld` OR `n_items==0`; `ABSTAIN_STATUSES = {"insufficient","abstained","no_answer"}` (metrics.py:48 [code]). The arm adapter (`eval/v7/arms.py:897-918` [code]) sets `abstained = (status=="insufficient")` while `delivered = outcome.refs` regardless — so **`status=insufficient` WITH premise items delivered double-scores both correct-refusal and premise any@k**. That is the harness-optimal behavior.

**Industry convention** — cat-5 is generally *excluded* from headline LoCoMo QA accuracy: Mem0's own eval (`mem0ai/mem0` `evaluation/evals.py` [vendor]) does `if category == "5": continue`; the Mem0 paper reports only 4 categories. Hindsight (`vectorize-io/hindsight-benchmarks` README [vendor]): "We skipped the Adversarial category as it is almost impossible to evaluate reliably" (reports 83.2% overall excl. adversarial; also flags LoCoMo ground-truth speaker-attribution errors). The Zep rebuttal thread (`getzep/zep-papers` issue #5 [vendor]) treats "cats 1-4 only" as the fair convention — including cat-5 inflates scores for systems that reflexively refuse. Hebb Mind did measure adversarial-only end-to-end at 88.3% [vendor claim]. LongMemEval-V2's abstention judge (`xiaowu0162/LongMemEval-V2` `qa_eval_metrics.py` [vendor]): correct = identify the *specific premise flaw* consistent with the reference; bare "UNKNOWN/cannot determine" = 0; premise-following answers = 0 → argues for delivering flaw-identifying evidence over silence.

**Paper context** — LoCoMo (ACL 2024, https://aclanthology.org/2024.acl-long.747/, arXiv 2402.17753 [paper]): adversarial questions "mislead the model into hallucinating"; models are "especially prone to misassigning dialogs or events to the wrong speaker" — the constructed trap IS predominantly speaker re-binding; RAG does best when dialogue becomes "a database of assertions (observations) about each speaker's life" — i.e. per-speaker subject-bound assertions, which is exactly verbatim's `observations_v7`/`profiles_v7` design (schema_v7.py:497-528 [code]) — currently empty in the measured store [brief: orphan producers].

## B. Measured cat-5 structure (my analysis of locomo10.json)

- 446/1986 questions are cat-5 = 22.5% `[measured]`; the dev split's 213/990 = 21.5% `[brief]` matches.
- 443/446 name exactly one of the two conversation speakers `[measured]`. ~98.4% wh-initial, ~1.6% aux-initial `[measured]`.
- 2x2 grid on (asked speaker authors a gold premise turn) x (asked name appears in premise-turn text) `[measured]`:
  - (author=F, in-text=F): **224** — cleanest speaker-swap: named person never said AND isn't mentioned in the premise.
  - (author=F, in-text=T): **110** — named person is *talked about* in the premise turns but authored by the other speaker ("What did Caroline tell Melanie about her job?" → asked as if Melanie told).
  - (author=T, in-text=F): **110** — attribute-swap: asked speaker DID author premise turns, but the bound object/attribute differs.
  - (author=T, in-text=T): **1** — near-duplicate of an answerable question (label noise).
  - → **75% of cat-5 is detectable by pure speaker-binding**; the remaining 25% needs object/attribute binding.
- Answerable baseline (cats 1-4): the asked speaker IS among gold-evidence turn speakers in 1478/1540 = 96.0% `[measured]` (cat1 278/282 = 98.6%, cat2 312/321 = 97.2%, cat3 90/96 = 93.8%, cat4 798/841 = 94.9%); absent = 62/1540 = 4.0%. Cat-5: asked speaker is a gold-evidence speaker in only 112/446 = 25.1% `[measured]`. So "asked speaker absent from evidence speakers" separates cat-5 at ~75% recall / ~4% FPR *under gold evidence*. Retrieved pools will differ — treat as a verdict signal, not an eligibility gate.
- R13 `abstain_likely` coverage: `intent_v2.py:535-557` [code] requires ever-verification frames ("have i ever", "did i mention", "is there any"), aux-initial + first/second-person (`_ABSTAIN_PERSON` = pronouns + "there" — third-person names like "Caroline" fail at line ~345), or zero content terms — and wh-initial is excluded outright (line ~538). Real cat-5: ~98% wh-initial + aux frames about third persons → **R13 fires on ~0 of 446 cat-5** `[measured × code]`. Trigger (d) never sees its required `abstain_likely` intent.

## D. No-LLM detection recipe (cheapest-first, all SQLite+regex)

1. **Query-shape gate**: wh-initial + ≥1 person canon resolved to `speaker_canon` namespace + intent not already `factual_lookup` short-circuit. This is a *new* premise-check flag, NOT a widening of R13 — R13's first/second-person restriction is correct for "did I ever" self-memory; cat-5 needs third-person premise checks.
2. **Speaker-binding check (covers ~75%)**: for each verdict group, test `q_speaker ∈ {u.speaker_canon for supporting units}`. If q_speaker resolves AND ≥1 group has support AND no supporting unit was authored by q_speaker → emit `negative_evidence` + `premise_mismatch=speaker` on that group. Cost: zero new SQL — `speaker_canon` is already fetched for candidates and `q_speaker` is already resolved once [code]. Refinement for the (F,T) mentioned-not-author bucket (110 tasks): require the asked name to be absent from `role=subject` mentions — until entity_mentions lands, proxy = asked canon appears in quote text but unit speaker differs AND quote lacks first-person co-mention ("I" tokens) — heuristic, keep it a `partial` label not full negative.
3. **Object/attribute binding (covers ~25%)**: secondary query entity canons (e.g. "grandpa") must co-occur in the supporting candidates' quote text (byte-check on already-materialized quotes) or in entity_mentions — absent everywhere → `binding_missing` facet → negative. Needs entity_mentions populated for full coverage; quote-text fallback works today.
4. **Negation evidence**: `polarity(quote) == NEGATED` in a token window around asked predicate terms → `negative_evidence` (NegEx, Chapman 2001 [paper]; verbatim already has the classifier [code]).
5. **No-SUPPORTED-guard**: trigger (d) already requires no SUPPORTED group [code] — that bounds the ~5.8% measured FPR: if a group is genuinely supported, deliver it and don't abstain.

Cost: steps 2-4 are O(R·|quote|) regexes + in-memory set checks over data S7 already touched — est. <2ms added vs the current ~1ms structural pass `[infer]`; nothing hits the deadline budget.

## E. Abstain vs deliver-labeled vs correct — what each costs

- **Deliver premise items + `status=insufficient`**: scores BOTH `correct_refusal` (withheld via ABSTAIN_STATUSES ∪ n_items) AND premise `any@k` under this harness [code metrics.py:292-300, arms.py:897-918]; matches official semantics (refusal string answer-side + premise recall evidence-side) [vendor]; gives the downstream answerer the material to *name the flaw* (LME judge requires it [vendor]). Facade already ships `items` under `status=insufficient` on the v7 engine (facade.py:2648-2742 [code]); pack assembly ships labeled items under any verdict (pipeline.py:1108-1289 [code]).
- **Pure abstain (n_items=0)**: same correct_refusal credit, but forfeits all premise any@k (cat-5 is 21.5% of the corpus → permanent ~0.2×(premise-recall) hole in overall any@10) and leaves the answerer nothing to refuse *with*.
- **Auto-correction** (rewrite "Melanie"→"Caroline"): attractive on paper; without `entity_mentions`/subject roles it guesses wrong — the 110 (F,T) and 110 (T,F) buckets are NOT symmetric speaker swaps `[measured]`; correction needs subject-binding to know WHICH participant to substitute. Recommend emit `premise_correction` as a *label* ("evidence attributes the claim to X"), never a rewritten retrieval target.
- **Labeling** (`premise_suspect`/`speaker_mismatch`/`binding_missing` on group verdicts + INSUFFICIENT status) is strictly better than silent delivery: eval counts the same refs, and the answerer sees the flaw stated.

## Expected verdict shift

- Today: cat-5 any@10 = 0.089 item / 0.465 session `[brief]`; correct_refusal on cat-5 ≈ abstain_rate ~0.03-0.13 (owned-twins measured 0.125 on abstain slice, `eval/v7/results/v7_trackr_post_integration.json` [measured]; dev split likely similar `[infer]`). Trigger (d) cannot fire — R13 misses ~all wh-initial cat-5 AND nothing emits negative_evidence `[measured × code]`.
- With the speaker-binding negative signal alone: ~75% of cat-5 gains an `INSUFFICIENT` verdict (334/446 measured absent-author) while still delivering premise items → correct_refusal(cat-5) → ~0.7-0.8 `[calculated from measured 334/446]`; any@10 unchanged→better once pool/deadline fixes land (premise turns are high-overlap targets — the trap was generated from them [paper]).
- With binding check (L3) added: coverage → ~95%+ of cat-5 `[infer: 224+110+110+1×recovery]` at bounded FPR under the no-SUPPORTED guard.
- Overall any@10 lift: cat-5 = 21.5% of dev tasks `[brief]`; recovering premise recall to ~0.45 item-level adds ~+0.07-0.09 overall `[calculated]`; correct_refusal(cat-5) is the headline movement 0.03-0.13 → ~0.7-0.8.
- Official-answerer end-to-end: an answerer that says "not mentioned" iff `status==insufficient` goes cat-5 QA accuracy ~0 → ~0.75-0.9 vs the official substring scorer [calculated from measured coverage × vendor scoring rule].

## Verdicts

1. **Emit `negative_evidence`/`premise_mismatch=speaker` when the resolved query speaker authored zero supporting units in a group** — zero new SQL (reuses fused `speaker_canon` + once-computed `q_speaker`), covers ~75% of cat-5 `[measured: 334/446 absent-author]` at ~4% FPR `[measured: 62/1540 answerable absent-author]`, bounded by the existing no-SUPPORTED guard. Expected lift: correct_refusal(cat-5) 0.03-0.13→~0.7-0.8; cost <2ms in S7. **Highest value-per-line in the project.**
2. **Keep delivering premise items under INSUFFICIENT — do not withhold delivery** — required for double-scoring under this harness AND for official premise-evidence recall AND for LME-style flaw-identification. Zero cost; it's already the v7 path (facade.py:2648-2742, pipeline.py:1108-1289). No change needed, just don't regress it.
3. **Add a `speaker`/`binding` facet to `MissingDescriptor`** and label the group's missing evidence `premise suspect` — the descriptor plumbing exists (verdict_v2.py:~1060-1088); cheap [infer].
4. **Extend object-binding only after `entity_mentions`/`profiles_v7` producers are unwedged** (they're orphan code [brief]) — until then use quote-text co-occurrence of secondary query canons as the weak check; do NOT ship auto-correction; emit `premise_correction` labels at most.
5. **Do NOT widen R13 `abstain_likely` to wh-initial** — its first/second-person frame is correct for self-memory questions; cat-5 needs the third-person premise check above as a separate signal feeding the same trigger-(d) negative-evidence path (verdict_v2.py:1144-1155). Widening R13 would false-positive the 93.6% of answerable questions that ARE wh-initial about a named speaker `[measured]`.
6. **Report cat-5 as two numbers everywhere** — `correct_refusal(cat-5)` and `premise any@k(cat-5)` — and exclude cat-5 from headline QA accuracy comparisons with Mem0/Hindsight (both vendors exclude it [vendor]); include it in retrieval-recall comparisons since premise turns are legitimate gold.
7. **Watch the (T,F) attribute-swap bucket (110/446 = 25%)** — speaker check misses it by construction; only subject/object binding catches it; it's the reason full coverage waits on the entity/event producers landing.
